Shifting Winds, Enduring Roots

Producer & Director: Shihan Zhang

After moving to the United States, I unexpectedly connected with an aunt I had never met. We came from the same village and shared only a distant blood tie, yet a Thanksgiving invitation made us feel like family. It left me wondering: what keeps us bound across oceans and generations? This film follows that question through three generations of Hakka women: in my grandmother’s courtyard house in China, at my aunt’s dining table in America, and in my cousin’s contemporary life. The Hakka dialect is fading and clan ties are weakening, yet being Hakka has never meant preserving a fixed tradition. It is a story of migration and adaptation, of people who leave home, build new lives, and carry forward family, resilience, and belonging. Rather than a nostalgic portrait, the film asks what is lost, what changes, and what quietly remains. I’m drawn to the subtler forms of inheritance found in everyday moments: how we care for family, adapt to displacement, and carry home with us. It is one family’s story, but I hope it speaks to anyone searching for home and the invisible threads that connect us.

The Silver Cup

ASIN: B0HLGPN6BG

Synopsis

The Hakka-Punti Clan Wars have left the circular fortress-home diminished and thirty-one men dead. Arriving into this world of careful silences is Katharine Meurer, a German missionary physician whose rigorous training meets a community held together by women. What develops between Katharine and Wenxiu, the tulou's spirit medium, is a relationship built in part Hakka, part botanical Latin, and part unnamable language. Told across fifteen chapters and two long poems, The Silver Cup is a work of historical literary fiction about the knowledge women hold when excluded from power, and what survives when the record chooses what to remember and what to leave behind.
